The Iris flower dataset is one of the most famous datasets in machine learning.

It was introduced by Ronald Fisher in 1936 as an example of linear discriminant analysis.
The dataset contains 150 samples from three species of Iris flowers:
- Iris setosa
- Iris versicolor
- Iris virginica

Each sample has four features measured in centimetres:
- Sepal length
- Sepal width
- Petal length
- Petal width

Setosa is easily separable from the other two species based on petal length and width.
Versicolor and virginica overlap somewhat and are harder to distinguish.

The dataset is commonly used for testing classification algorithms including:
k-nearest neighbours, support vector machines, decision trees, and neural networks.

Fisher collected the data from the Gaspe Peninsula in Quebec, Canada.
All measurements are in centimetres and represent real morphological measurements.
